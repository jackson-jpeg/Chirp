ChirpChirps has no accounts, no sign-in and no servers: nearby devices connect directly over Bluetooth and peer-to-peer Wi-Fi, so nothing a user shares leaves the devices around them.

This build addresses Guideline 5.1.2(i). Everything below is testable on one device in Demo Mode.

DEMO MODE (the Simulator has no Bluetooth, so a real mesh needs hardware)
Settings > Demo Mode, or "Try Demo Mode" on any empty screen. Four simulated people appear with chat history, voice messages, map pins, push-to-talk replies, and entries in Friends and Diagnostics, so every path below is reachable. A "DEMO" badge with a one tap exit sits on every screen. Demo traffic is generated on device and blocked at the transport layer: nothing is transmitted or saved.

1. USERS CAN BLOCK OTHER USERS
Block is reachable everywhere a person appears. Except on the map it is a press and hold:
  Map > tap a pin
  Messages > open a channel > press and hold a message
  Messages > open a channel > Talk > press and hold a peer in the participant strip
  Messages > Friends "See All" > press and hold a person
  Talk > press and hold the mesh strip at the bottom (opens Diagnostics) > press and hold a node
  Voice Messages (waveform icon, top left) > press and hold a received message
Each opens the same sheet, headed with the person's callsign and fingerprint, offering Block and Report. Block asks one confirmation. Settings > Blocked Users lists everyone blocked, with Unblock.

A block is keyed to the peer's cryptographic identity, not their display name, so renaming does not evade it. Blocking drops everything from that peer on arrival: push-to-talk audio, voice messages, text and position. Their history is hidden and their pin disappears. They also stop receiving the blocker's position, which is encrypted per recipient, so a blocked peer cannot read one even when relaying it.

2. PERMISSION BEFORE LOCATION IS SHOWN, AND IT CAN BE DECLINED
Map > Check In. On a fresh install this shows the iOS location prompt directly, with no screen of ours in front of it. Declining leaves the app fully usable; the Map shows a notice with an Open Settings button.

After iOS grants permission, the app asks separately whether to share: a sheet headed "Show your location to nearby peers?" with two equally prominent buttons, "Share for 15 Minutes" and "Don't Share". Don't Share leaves the user off the map; swiping the sheet away is also a decline. "About location sharing", linked from that sheet and from Settings, grants nothing.

3. CHECK IN IS MANUAL, EVERY TIME
The consent sheet appears on every Check In, including the second in one session. Nothing is remembered: no "don't ask again", no automatic or always-on option, no background sharing, no setting that shares continuously. Relaunching always begins checked out. A session lasts 15 minutes and ends on its own, on Stop, or when the app leaves the foreground.

REPORTING AND FILTERING (Guideline 1.2)
Report sits beside Block on the same sheet, from every path in section 1. It blocks the peer and opens a prefilled email to support@chirpchirps.com with the reported device's fingerprint, a reason picker and, where the report came from a message, its text. With no servers, email is the reporting channel. Terms of Use at https://chirpchirps.com/terms state zero tolerance for objectionable content and abusive users and commit to acting within 24 hours; agreement is required at onboarding.

Incoming messages are screened on device and collapsed behind "Hidden message, tap to show", on by default; toggle at Settings > Message Filter.

PERMISSIONS (5.1.1)
Microphone: requested at the end of onboarding, on a screen whose only button is Continue, which shows the iOS prompt. Whatever the answer the app works; if access is off, Talk shows a notice with an Open Settings button.
Location: as in section 2. No screen precedes either prompt; nothing is requested at launch.
