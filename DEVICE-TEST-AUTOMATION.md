# ChirpChirp — automated device testing

Replaces the manual Part A of `DEVICE-SMOKE-TEST.md`. One command, two phones,
no button-pressing:

```
ios devicetest chirp
```

Exit codes are three-way and deliberately not collapsed:

| Code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `0` | every check passed |
| `1` | a check failed — audio did not cross, or crossed wrong |
| `2` | the run could not be completed (device missing, build failed, no telemetry) |

`2` is distinct from `0` on purpose. "The harness could not tell you" must never
present as "the app is fine" — that specific confusion has already cost this
project three rounds of false confidence.

---

## What you have to do

Plug both iPhones into the Mac, unlock them, tap **Trust This Computer** if
asked, and leave them connected, unlocked, and **a few feet from the Mac's
speakers** with the Mac's volume up. That last part is not incidental: the Mac
plays the test audio out loud and the phones hear it through their microphones.
That is the only way to exercise the real capture path.

Then leave them alone for about two minutes. Don't touch the screens — the
harness is driving them.

Check both are seen:

```
xcrun devicectl list devices        # on the Mac
```

Both must say **connected**. `unavailable` means not usable, and the harness
will stop with exit 2 rather than test one phone and call it a pass.

## How it works

Four pieces, each replaceable and each testable on its own.

**1. Actuation — `ChirpUITests/`.** XCUITest presses the real PTT button via
its accessibility identifier. Not a test-only hook into `PTTEngine`: the gesture
recogniser, the view state and the floor request are all things that can break,
and a harness that bypassed them would go green while the button did nothing.

**2. Coordination — a wall clock.** The two phones run as two separate
`xcodebuild test` processes on separate hardware. They cannot talk to each
other; the only channel between them is the mesh, which is the thing under test.
Coordinating over it would produce a harness that passes when the app works and
*hangs* when it doesn't. So the orchestrator hands both runs the same absolute
start time before either begins, and each role knows what it should be doing at
every moment. A phone that crashes doesn't hang its partner.

**3. Measurement — `AudioTelemetry.swift`.** Debug-only. Records an RMS
envelope at both ends of the audio path: what the microphone captured, and what
was handed to the player node. Timestamped in absolute time so the sender's
envelope can be compared against the receiver's.

It is inside `#if DEBUG`, so it does not exist in a Release build, and even in
Debug it is inert unless launched with `-ChirpAudioTelemetry YES`, which only
the harness passes. It holds its state in a `Mutex` rather than behind an
`@unchecked Sendable` — the escape-hatch count did not move.

**4. Judgement — `lib/devicetest/analyze.py`** (in `ios-toolkit`). Off-device,
because it needs both phones' data and because assertion logic that lives off
the phone can be tested without one.

### Why the signal is speech and the measure is correlation

The audio session runs in `.voiceChat` mode, which applies hardware echo
cancellation, automatic gain control and **noise suppression**. A steady tone is
exactly what noise suppression is designed to remove — a tone-based test would
have measured the suppressor, not the mesh. The stimulus is therefore real
speech, generated by macOS's own synthesiser.

That rules out comparing waveforms. Opus compression, resampling, AGC and the
jitter buffer all mean the far-side samples are legitimately different. What
must survive is the **shape over time**: speech loud here is speech loud there,
a fraction of a second later. So the measure is the peak Pearson correlation
between the two envelopes, searched across plausible latencies.

Three independent measures, because no one of them catches everything:

| Measure | Catches |
|---|---|
| Level floor | silence — muted output, dead playback path |
| Correlation | garble — audio arriving that isn't the sender's signal |
| **Coverage** | dropouts — chunks missing |

Coverage exists because of a finding, not a hunch. Audio with **half of it
removed in half-second blocks** scores **0.55 correlation and passes** the 0.5
threshold, because the blocks that survive align perfectly — and its mean level
stays healthy for the same reason. Neither of the first two measures sees it.
`test_dropouts_fail` now pins that explicitly: it asserts correlation *passes*
and coverage *fails*, so if anyone deletes the coverage check the test explains
what it was for.

## Revert-proof

### Proven — the judgement

Every failure mode the harness claims to catch is constructed and asserted to
fail, at the same thresholds the real runs use. On the VPS:

```
$ python3 /root/ios-toolkit/lib/devicetest/test_analyze.py
  PASS  test_working_audio_passes                     corr=0.998 lag=0.34s
  PASS  test_silent_receiver_fails                    receiver produced silence...
  PASS  test_receiver_with_no_samples_at_all_fails    empty -> fail
  PASS  test_silent_sender_is_reported_as_a_sender_fault
  PASS  test_garbled_audio_fails                      corr=0.093, level ok (0.107)
  PASS  test_dropouts_fail                            corr=0.552 (passes!), coverage=53% (fails)
  PASS  test_stuck_microphone_fails_the_silence_check active->fail, quiet->pass
  PASS  test_constant_series_does_not_correlate       flat -> 0.0
  PASS  test_gaps_resample_to_zero_not_to_neighbours  gap preserved
  PASS  test_uncorrelated_noise_stays_well_below_the_threshold  worst of 20: 0.139
Executed 10 checks, 0 failures
EXIT=0
```

Muted output, dropped packets and garbled audio all fail, deterministically, in
under a second — no device, no app rebuild.

### Proven — the plumbing that can fail silently

* **One phone instead of two** → exit 2, not a pass.
* **Schedule drift.** The button-press times live in Swift; the stimulus
  playback times live in Python. If they drift, every run fails for a reason
  that looks like broken audio. The orchestrator greps the Swift file and
  refuses to start. Verified by changing `aTransmit` from 35 to 36:
  `schedule mismatch: aTransmit expected 35`, `Refusing to run`, exit 2.

### NOT yet proven — and this is the important part

**The two-phone run has never been executed.** The second iPhone shows as
`unavailable` to the Mac. What that leaves unproven:

* that the telemetry file is actually written on a device and pulled off it
* that the wall-clock choreography keeps two phones in step in practice
* that audio crosses at all, which is the entire question

So: the judgement is proven, the refusal paths are proven, and the signing path
is proven (a UI test runs on hardware, exit 0). The end-to-end is **written but
unrun**. Plug in the second phone and the first real run will tell us — and if
it fails, the failure will be attributable, which is the point.

## What CANNOT be automated

A short accurate list, as asked. These need eyes or hardware.

| # | Not automatable | Why | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Sound actually leaving the speaker** | The strongest claim software can make is "the app handed this signal to the OS to play". Volume, silent switch, routing to a dead output and hardware faults all live past that line. | One 30-second human listen before submission. |
| 2 | **Phone call / Siri interruption** | XCUITest cannot place an inbound call or invoke Siri on the device under test. | Manual, once. Call the phone mid-transmission. |
| 3 | **Headset / route change** | Physically plugging and unplugging. | Manual, once. |
| 4 | **Walking out of range** | Requires physically moving a phone away. Airplane mode is not equivalent — it drops the radio instantly rather than degrading. | Manual, in Part B. |
| 5 | **Screen lock** | No API to lock the device from XCUITest. Backgrounding via Home *is* automated and covers most of defect #1. | Manual, once. |
| 6 | **Three-device stale holder** | Two phones exist. | Stays DEVICE-UNVERIFIED. |
| 7 | **Part B — floor control** | Gated on the FloorController rebuild landing. | After the rebuild. |

Items 1–3 and 5 collapse into **one five-minute human session before
submission**: launch, listen, call the phone mid-transmission, plug in
headphones, lock and unlock. That is the residual. Everything else in Part A is
now a command.

## Troubleshooting

**"Need two CONNECTED iPhones; found 1"** — the other phone is asleep, locked,
or hasn't been trusted. Unlock and re-plug.

**"No telemetry from at least one phone"** — the app didn't record. Either it
never launched, or it wasn't launched by the harness (telemetry is off unless
`-ChirpAudioTelemetry YES` is passed). Check
`/tmp/chirp-devicetest-{A,B}.log` on the Mac.

**"sender captured silence"** — the phone's microphone produced nothing. Usually
the Mac's volume is down or the phones are too far from its speakers. This is
reported as a *sender* fault specifically so it is never mistaken for a
receiver or transport failure.

**Signing failures** — the runner has its own bundle ID
(`com.jacksonmsanger.chirpchirp.uitests.xctrunner`), registered in the portal
and in `fastlane/Matchfile`. If its profile expires: `ios certs refresh chirp`.
